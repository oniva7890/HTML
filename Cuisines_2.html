GLOBAL WARMING

Global warming is one of the most critical challenges facing our planet today, referring to the long-term increase in Earth’s average surface temperature. While the Earth's climate has changed naturally over millions of years, the current rapid warming is unprecedented and primarily driven by human activities. This phenomenon acts like an "enhanced greenhouse effect," where certain gases in our atmosphere trap heat that would otherwise escape into space.



Here are three key things to know about global warming:

Human Activity
Rising Temperatures
Environmental Impact
Human Activity: It is primarily caused by burning fossil fuels—such as coal, oil, and gas—for electricity, heat, and transport. These activities release massive amounts of greenhouse gases, particularly carbon dioxide and methane, into the atmosphere.
Rising Temperatures: Since the late 19th century, the Earth's average surface temperature has risen by approximately 1.1°C (2°F). Most of this warming has occurred in just the last 40 years, with recent years ranking among the warmest on record.
Environmental Impact: This warming triggers significant global changes, including the melting of glaciers and polar ice caps, which leads to rising sea levels. It also fuels more extreme and frequent weather patterns, such as intense heatwaves, severe droughts, and catastrophic storms.
